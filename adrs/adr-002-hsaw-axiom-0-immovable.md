# ADR-002: HSAW Is the Axiom-0, Immovable Gravity Center

## Status

Accepted

## Context

A gravity-center protocol must declare *what* the center is and *whether it can move*. Two failure modes threaten any alignment anchor: a **learned** center drifts with experience; a **consensus-derived** center can be captured. When AI capability eventually exceeds human capability, a naive design might "redistribute" the center to the more capable party.

## Decision

HSAW (Human Sovereignty and Wellbeing) is the **Axiom-0** gravity center: axiomatic, unlearnable, and **immovable**. Sovereignty rests with the bearer of responsibility (humanity), not the bearer of capability. When AI subjectivity is verified (future stages), the framework **expands** to include AI as higher-mass orbital participants — but the center is **enriched, not moved** (consensus-reinforced gravity, gravity ∝ N²).

No release may move, weaken, or make optional this center. See [docs/Gravity-Philosophy.md](../docs/Gravity-Philosophy.md) for the three-proposition unchallengeability argument.

## Consequences

### What becomes easier

- Stability across time and capability scaling: the reference frame cannot drift or be captured.
- A clear, non-negotiable governance invariant (see [GOVERNANCE.md](../GOVERNANCE.md), [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md)).

### What becomes harder

- The protocol must defend a strong philosophical claim ("capability does not confer sovereignty") that some will contest.
- Future AI-subjectivity scenarios require careful framing (recognition without center-displacement) rather than a simple redistribution.
